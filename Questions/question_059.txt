In 1944, a chemical plant in Saskatchewan invented a novel process for producing ammonia that had to operate under extreme winter conditions. The process involves three simultaneous equilibrium reactions:

N2 (g) + 3H2 (g) ⇌ 2NH3 (g)     ΔH = -92 kJ/mol
N2O4 (g) ⇌ 2NO2 (g)             ΔH = +57 kJ/mol
2NO2 (g) + 2NH3 (g) ⇌ N2 (g) + 2H2O (g) + N2O4 (g)   ΔH = -125 kJ/mol

If the initial partial pressures are:
P(N2) = 2.0 atm
P(H2) = 6.0 atm
P(NO2) = 0.5 atm
P(N2O4) = 0.3 atm
P(NH3) = 0.1 atm
P(H2O) = 0.0 atm

And the temperature is -25°C, explain how the system will shift to reach equilibrium. In your analysis, consider:
1. The effect of Saskatchewan's cold climate on each equilibrium
2. The direction each reaction will shift based on Le Chatelier's principle
3. How the shifts in one reaction affect the others
4. The final qualitative distribution of products vs reactants

Provide your reasoning for each step.
